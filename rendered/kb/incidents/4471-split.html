one-day: its 5 reports stay counted under parser, where the desk filed them by tenant.

Counted the desk’s way, as CauseTable counts the export: each report once (the export repeats some ids), under the cause its tenant points to. The one-day reports keep their place under parser and also get their own row and ticket.

parser: the shared parser reads day-first dates month first

A tenant whose locale writes the day first (every tenant on file) sends a date like 07/04, and the parser reads 4 July. The report shows a day/month swap: “Booked for 7 April. The invite says 4 July.” It comes in through the app, by email and through booking-v2’s form, whose hint says DD/MM/YYYY. This is the bug #4471 describes, and the fix that is queued.

hint: booking-v3’s form tells day-first tenants to type month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Day-first tenants on booking-v3 (Alder Physio, Copper Studio, Pale Harbour) follow the hint, type the month first, and the form reads the day first. The report looks like a parser swap, but only reports sent through that form have it. A parser fix does not touch it: the hint must match the tenant’s locale, or the form must read what the hint asks for.

one-day: dates move by one day, not by a swap

These reports describe a date one day off, or two places seeing two different days for the same booking: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” A day/month swap cannot do that. It points to a time-zone or daylight-saving bug in how invites are sent. The desk had already listed these reports as read differently (desk-notes.json) and counts them under parser by tenant. They stay counted there; their ticket is their own.

How to tell them apart

  • Look the tenant up, not the reporter’s browser. Intake’s tag comes from the browser locale and is wrong for many of these reports.
  • Booking-v3 tenant, sent through the form: hint. Any other day-first tenant: parser.
  • Read the body before you merge. A one-day move is one-day, whatever the tenant.

Lessons

  • A subject line names the symptom, not the cause. Merge on cause.
  • A footnote is not a ticket. When the body disagrees with the tag, open one.
